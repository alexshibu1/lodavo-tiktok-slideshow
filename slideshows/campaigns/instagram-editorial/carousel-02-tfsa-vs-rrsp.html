TAX STRATEGY

TFSA vs RRSP: Which One Should You Max First?

The answer depends on your income, your goals, and when you need the money.

›
ACCOUNT #1

The TFSA

Tax-Free Savings Account

  • Contribute with after-tax dollars
  • All growth + withdrawals are 100% tax-free
  • 2024 limit: $7,000
  • Lifetime room (age 18 since 2009): $95,000

Best for: Flexibility, any goal — emergency fund, vacation, car, house, retirement. Withdraw anytime without penalty.

›
ACCOUNT #2

The RRSP

Registered Retirement Savings Plan

  • Contribute with pre-tax dollars (tax refund now)
  • Growth is tax-sheltered until withdrawal
  • Withdrawals taxed as income
  • 2024 limit: 18% of income, max $31,560

Best for: High earners, retirement, first home via HBP ($35K withdrawal for a down payment).

›

Head-to-Head Comparison

TFSA RRSP
Tax on withdrawal None Yes
Tax deduction now No Yes
Flexibility High Low
Govt benefit impact None Reduces
Room for high earners $7K/yr $31.5K/yr
First home (HBP) No $35K

TFSA wins on flexibility. RRSP wins on tax savings for high earners.

›
THE ANSWER

For Most Young Canadians: TFSA First

If you earn under $55K, max your TFSA first. Here's why:

  • Your RRSP tax refund is small at low brackets (20.5% federal)
  • TFSA withdrawals don't reduce GIS, CCB, or GST credit
  • You can withdraw anytime without penalty or tax
  • Room comes back the following year

Above $55K? The RRSP deduction becomes more valuable — but TFSA flexibility still matters.
