TFSA — Tax-Free Savings Account
$7,000/YEAR
Lifetime Maximum
$95,000
If you were 18 in 2009. Yours may be less — check CRA My Account.
Tax-Free Growth
All investment gains inside the TFSA — interest, dividends, capital gains — are never taxed.
Tax-Free Withdrawals
Take money out any time. No tax. Room comes back January 1 next year.
Think of it as: tax paid upfront, freedom forever

R
RRSP — Registered Retirement Plan
18% OF INCOME
Or $32,490 max (2025) — whichever is less
Tax Deduction
Contributions lower your taxable income. If you earn $60K and contribute $5K, you're taxed on $55K.
Tax-Deferred Growth
Investments grow tax-free inside. You pay tax when you withdraw — ideally in retirement when your rate is lower.
Home Buyers' Plan
$60,000
Withdraw tax-free for your first home. Repay over 15 years.
Think of it as: tax deferred now, paid in retirement

T
USE THE TFSA WHEN
01
You earn under $55K
The RRSP tax deduction isn't worth much in a low bracket. TFSA gives you more flexibility.
02
You want access anytime
No penalties. No tax on withdrawal. Room comes back next year. True freedom.
03
Already have employer RRSP match
Max the match (free money!), then put the rest in your TFSA.
Bottom line: TFSA first for most young Canadians.
4 / 8
R
USE THE RRSP WHEN
01
You earn over $55K
Higher tax bracket = bigger deduction. A $5K contribution saves you ~$1,500 in tax.
02
You want a tax refund now
RRSP contributions are deductible. Use the refund to invest in your TFSA (pro move).
03
Buying your first home
Home Buyers' Plan: withdraw up to $60K tax-free. Repay over 15 years.
Bottom line: RRSP shines when your income is higher.

WITHDRAWAL RULES
The biggest difference between the two
TFSA
Withdraw
Anytime
No tax. No penalty. No questions.
Room
Returns Jan 1
Withdraw $5K in June, re-contribute $5K in January.
Tax Impact
None
Does not affect benefits or credits.
RRSP
Withdraw
Taxed as income
Added to your income that year. Could bump your bracket.
Room
Gone forever
Withdraw $5K and you never get that $5K room back.
Tax Impact
Significant
Can reduce GIS, OAS, and other benefits.

AGE RULES & TIMELINE
When each one starts, ends, and converts
TFSA
Starts at age 18
Room accumulates from the year you turn 18. No income required. Open one the day you can.
RRSP
Needs earned income, no age min
Room = 18% of previous year's earned income. You need a T4 or self-employment income to build room.
TFSA
No end date, no conversion
Yours for life. Keep contributing, keep withdrawing. No forced changes ever.
RRSP
Converts to RRIF at age 71
Must convert by Dec 31 of the year you turn 71. Minimum mandatory withdrawals begin (taxed).
